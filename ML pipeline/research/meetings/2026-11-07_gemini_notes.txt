Initial Insurtech and Payment Brainstorming: Harsh Pandhe opened the discussion
on the fintech hackathon problem statement (00:27:02). Sumit Sanjay Shinde
proposed monitoring country-level data to automatically trigger instant PayNow
payouts—Singapore's equivalent to India's UPI—when specific thresholds are breached
(00:28:11). Pyae Sone suggested an insurance model for delivery riders bundled into
orders for 8 to 10 cents, triggering daily automated payouts when financial thresholds
are met (00:30:19). Ankita Sagar recommended using digital twins to test these
payment systems. Sumit noted that PayNow is not directly accessible on day one,
requiring simulation during the pitch (00:29:08) (00:31:27).



Target Audience Selection Strategy: Ankita Sagar questioned whether to target all
three audience groups or focus on a single one. Pyae Sone and Sumit Sanjay Shinde
agreed that narrowing the focus ensures a better quality product (00:31:27). Sumit
explained that outdoor platform workers in Singapore are already largely protected by
platform companies like Grab, whereas micro, small, and medium enterprises,
specifically hawkers, neighborhood shops, and home-based businesses, remain the
most underserved segment (00:35:36).




Environmental Impact and Insurance Gaps: Pyae Sone identified a gap in existing
insurance policies, noting that environmental factors like haze reduce customer traffic
for hawkers without providing loss-of-business compensation (00:39:14). ARIEL ONG
and Sumit Sanjay Shinde discussed contrasting data on haze, noting that while
Indonesia saw fewer orders during haze, Singapore actually reported an increase in
food delivery orders (00:40:31).



Centralized Data-Driven Insurance Layer: Harsh Pandhe proposed creating a
centralized, data-driven insurance layer utilizing environmental metrics such as
Pollutant Standards Index thresholds of 100, 150, and 200, temperatures, and rainfall,
predicted using historical data and weather forecasts to calculate automated
insurance triggers and premiums (00:41:46). Ankita Sagar added that the system could
integrate a multi-objective hybrid solver utilizing adaptive local search, robust
optimization, and parallel computing to handle large-scale simulations and traffic
(00:44:20).
Task Allocation and Pre-Hackathon Development Plan: Harsh Pandhe outlined
team roles, assigning ARIEL ONG and Sumit Sanjay Shinde to payments, Pyae Sone and
Ankita Sagar to machine learning, Nancy Srivastava to the pitch, and Harsh to
supporting tasks (00:46:09). Harsh suggested building a complete mobile app and
launching it via an existing Play Store console account rather than a website to provide
judges with a tangible product (00:47:10) (00:49:33). Ankita and Nancy agreed, noting
they develop solutions prior to hackathons—similar to their approach in the Smart
India Hackathon—and use the 36-hour hackathon window strictly for judge feedback,
debugging, and prompt adjustments, with Harsh mentioning Claude could be used to
alter git push histories if necessary (00:48:21).



Critical Assessment of Platform Worker Insurance Models: ARIEL ONG raised a
design challenge regarding platform worker insurance, pointing out that Singapore
experiences year-round heat and rain, delivery demand surges during rainy days due to
platform rain surge bonuses, and delivery riders continue working through extreme
weather because they are paid per delivery without paid leave (00:50:39). Sumit Sanjay
Shinde concurred that platform workers do not stop working during adverse weather,
whereas hawkers experience reduced business during extreme events (00:52:58).



Hawker Demographics and Cybersecurity Integration: Sumit Sanjay Shinde
clarified that Singapore hawker centers consist of large food courts housing
independent, family-run micro-shops. ARIEL ONG noted that hawker locations differ,
with non-air-conditioned hawker centers losing more customers during haze
compared to air-conditioned ones (00:54:22). Ankita Sagar suggested incorporating
cybersecurity features such as penetration testing, referencing a shared document
outlining security ideas, which Pyae Sone confirmed experience with (00:56:25).



Final Selection of Target User Segment: Sumit Sanjay Shinde highlighted that
hawker demographics skew older and less tech-savvy, necessitating simple user
interfaces and explainable machine learning models (00:57:47). ARIEL ONG evaluated
the three target categories—eliminating households due to existing government
insurance overlap, raising reservations about platform workers due to their high
earnings during bad weather, and ultimately selecting micro, small, and medium
enterprises like hawkers. Pyae Sone and the rest of the team expressed agreement
with focusing on this third category (00:59:23).



Target Audience and Selection of Hawkers in Singapore: The team discussed
identifying vulnerable groups for insurance, noting that while app drivers are covered,
hawkers lack protection. Sumit Sanjay Shinde suggested that hawkers require
government mandates or backing to adopt solutions (01:00:31). Harsh Pandhe inquired
about the number of hawkers, prompting Sumit to state that there are over 6,000
stalls, contrasted with Harsh's mention of 30,000 drivers. ARIEL ONG argued that
hawkers need insurance most because extreme weather events like haze severely
impact dine-in and delivery losses, making impact more important than sheer quantity
(01:01:40). Harsh subsequently searched Google and referenced a figure of 14,000.
The team reached a consensus to prioritize an impact-focused approach targeting
hawkers (01:02:57).



Problem Statement Scope and Competitor Analysis: The discussion shifted to
whether the target scope was limited to Singapore or included India, where millions of

Singapore (01:04:39). Sumit added that hawkers are uniquely protected under
UNESCO heritage culture in Singapore. Ankita suggested evaluating competitors to

differentiation (01:06:25).




Data Availability and Phased Category Approach: Harsh Pandhe proposed
expanding the horizon beyond the three provided user categories, but Nancy

one category at a time to ensure proper depth. Addressing data availability, Nancy
inquired about climate data accessibility on GIS or other platforms, and Harsh
confirmed that decade-old data is easily obtainable. Ankita noted that data is widely
available from the provided data sets, with Sumit Sanjay Shinde mentioning Singapore's
government data portal, data.gov. The team agreed to sequentially target the provided
categories using readily available government climate data (01:07:40).



Machine Learning Strategy and Model Comparison: Ankita Sagar raised the
problem that standard weather prediction and forecasting models are common and
lack uniqueness for the hackathon. Nancy Srivastava proposed testing smaller models
alongside ensembles and creating a comprehensive model comparison table to clearly
demonstrate performance differences, advantages, and drawbacks compared to
other teams (01:09:03). Sumit Sanjay Shinde summarized the technical direction to
include unique approaches beyond weather forecasting and rigorous model testing.
ARIEL ONG emphasized diving deep into the core problem to ensure the solution's
quality (01:10:32). The consensus was to avoid generic weather forecasting by
implementing diverse model testing and comparative analysis (01:09:03).



Problem Requirements Compliance and Meeting Logistics: Ankita Sagar stressed

GitHub repository and upload the relevant data sets for the team (01:17:42).
