2. Findings: Focus A - Willingness to Pay and Take-Up
2.1 Macroeconomic and Demographic Baseline of the Target Segment
The development of a parametric insurance add-on for hawkers and platform workers in
Singapore requires a precise understanding of their constrained liquidity environments. Data
provided by the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) and the Department of Statistics Singapore
(SingStat) demonstrates a stark income divergence between gig economy participants and the
broader resident workforce. In 2025, the median gross monthly income from employment for
full-time employed residents (including employer CPF contributions) stood at SGD 5,775,
representing an annualized growth rate of 3.9% over the preceding decade (Verified)2.
Conversely, platform workers operate in a substantially lower income tier. In 2021, the median
gross monthly income for the approximately 73,600 platform workers was merely SGD 1,500
(Verified)1. Sector-specific segmentation reveals that delivery workers earned a median of SGD
1,800, while taxi drivers earned SGD 1,200 (Verified)1. Demographically, this segment is highly
vulnerable: over 50% of platform workers are aged 50 and above, and nearly 80% possess only
non-tertiary educational qualifications (Verified)1.
The transboundary haze event of October 2026 brutally exposed this vulnerability. The
Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) remained in the "Unhealthy" or "Very Unhealthy" range for 12
consecutive days, peaking at a 24-hour PSI of 230 on October 14 and October 22 (Verified)4.
While Singapore's macroeconomic supply chains remained intact (Verified)28, consumer-facing
micro-SMEs suffered catastrophic demand destruction. Food and beverage outlets,
particularly hawkers lacking indoor climate control, reported business volume declines
between 70% and 80% (Reported)7. These micro-enterprises, operating on thin margins and
facing rising baseline operational costs, cannot self-insure against consecutive days of lost
footfall, underscoring the urgent market necessity for parametric income protection.

2.2 Theoretical Demand Constraints: Pre-Payment Aversion and Risk
Aversion
Despite the acute need for income smoothing, selling standalone microinsurance to this
demographic faces formidable behavioral economics barriers. Research from J-PAL, the World
Bank, and leading economists (Cole, Cai, Banerjee) consistently indicates that demand for
index insurance in lower-income demographics is highly price-elastic and often collapses at
actuarially fair market prices (Verified)11.
The primary friction is compound risk aversion applied to the concept of basis risk. Basis
risk—the probability that a policyholder suffers a true economic loss but the parametric index
fails to trigger a payout (a false negative)—transforms the insurance product from a
guaranteed safety net into a "probabilistic insurance" lottery (Verified)12. According to
Kahneman and Tversky's models, individuals do not behave according to expected utility
theory when facing risky prospects; compound risk aversion depresses the demand for
conventional index insurance contracts by an estimated 13 percentage points below what a
standard risk-averse model would predict (Estimate)12.
Furthermore, extreme liquidity constraints generate "pre-payment aversion." For a hawker or
delivery rider, allocating capital to an insurance premium today crowds out immediate, tangible
investments (e.g., purchasing ingredients or fuel). Because the payout is uncertain and
temporally distant, the immediate opportunity cost of the premium dominates the purchasing
decision (Verified)11. The World Bank explicitly warns against governments and development
agencies investing in the widespread expansion of standalone index insurance without
addressing these deep-seated behavioral barriers (Verified)32.
2.3 The "Spillover" Catalyst: Manufacturing Trust
While upfront demand is constrained, empirical research identifies a powerful catalyst for
long-term product adoption: local spillover effects. A seminal longitudinal study by Cole et al.
(2014) on the dynamics of demand for index insurance found that household demand is
extraordinarily sensitive to recent, visible payouts within their peer network. If an index
insurance payout of Rs 1,000 (roughly equivalent to 5 days of wage labor) is disbursed in an
individual's village, the probability that the household purchases insurance in the subsequent
season increases by an astounding 25 to 50 percentage points (Verified)13.
This phenomenon operates as a form of "confirmation bias" among consumers. Seeing a peer
receive an automated, frictionless cash transfer validates the product's efficacy, overriding the
theoretical fear of basis risk (Verified)13. Therefore, the go-to-market strategy for Income
Insurance must artificially manufacture these initial spillovers. By highly subsidizing the product
in Year 1 (e.g., via MAS Financial Sector Technology and Innovation grants) and engineering the
trigger thresholds to ensure at least one minor payout occurs during the introductory period,
the insurer can establish absolute product trust. Once the target demographic experiences the
seamless execution of a PayNow transfer following a hazy or rainy day, resistance to paying a
commercial premium in Year 2 will plummet.
2.4 Product Structuring: Embedded vs. Standalone and Price Banding
To eliminate the friction of a separate sales process, the parametric add-on must be deeply
embedded into existing digital ecosystems. Phase 1 targets hawkers via embedding the
product into Income Stallholder Insurance or SmartBiz360 as an opt-out or one-click add-on.
Phase 2 targets gig workers by integrating directly into platform digital wallets (akin to Igloo's
DANA rain protection in Indonesia), allowing premiums to be collected via micro-deductions
from daily earnings rather than lump-sum annual payments.

2.5 Recommended Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) Survey Design
To calibrate the precise premium and payout parameters without triggering hypothetical bias, a
rigorous evaluation protocol must be utilized.
   1.​ Target Sample: 1,000 verified hawkers (NEA license cross-reference) and gig workers.
   2.​ Methodology: A Multiple Price List (MPL) discrete choice experiment. Instead of asking
       "How much would you pay?", respondents are presented with a series of A/B choices.
   3.​ Framing: Respondents are primed with recent memory. "Recall October 14, 2026, when
       the PSI hit 230 and footfall dropped."
   4.​ Choice Architecture: Option A is standard indemnity insurance (requires adjusting,
       30-day wait). Option B is the parametric cover (instant PayNow, but explicitly
       acknowledges a 10% chance the weather station misses the rain). By iterating the price
       of Option B randomly across the sample, the insurer can precisely plot the
       downward-sloping demand curve and identify the optimal price elasticity point.

3. Findings: Focus B - Product Design and Basis Risk
3.1 Advanced Actuarial Indexing: Expectile vs. Quantile Regression
The commercial viability of parametric insurance relies entirely on minimizing basis risk. If a
hawker's physical stall is flooded, but the National Environment Agency (NEA) weather station 3
kilometers away records rainfall just below the payout threshold, the resulting "false negative"
destroys consumer trust and violates MAS Fair Dealing expectations (Verified)30.
Historically, academic literature in index-based contract design utilized quantile regression to
tailor payouts to extremes (Conradt et al., 2015) (Verified)14. Quantile regression relies on
minimizing absolute deviations, making it robust to outliers. However, quantiles are
fundamentally concerned with the frequency of a threshold breach, rather than the severity of
the loss in the tail.
Recent breakthroughs in stochastic modeling by Maier and Scherer (2025) demonstrate that
expectile regression serves as a strictly superior, basis-risk-optimal payment scheme for
parametric insurance (Verified)15. Expectiles are defined via asymmetric squared loss functions.
This mathematical property means that expectiles explicitly account for both the frequency of
the event and the magnitude of the tail severity (Verified)15.
For the Singapore context, a PSI of 105 (just over the unhealthy threshold) causes a marginal
drop in hawker footfall. A PSI of 288 (the peak recorded on Oct 14, 2026) causes total demand
collapse (Verified)6. An expectile-based algorithm structurally ensures that the payout curve
scales aggressively in non-linear proportion to the severity of the weather reading, drastically
reducing the out-of-pocket uninsured loss (FNP) for the policyholder compared to flat
threshold triggers.
3.2 Occupational Heat Stress (WBGT) Triggers
The design of the heat protection module must draw directly from the lived realities of outdoor
workers and recent legislative changes. The Self-Employed Women's Association (SEWA) heat
insurance pilot in India (2023–2025), designed alongside Swiss Re and Blue Marble, provides
the definitive operational blueprint (Verified)33. The critical lesson from SEWA's 2024 redesign
was that reliance on single daily maximum temperatures failed to capture the true hazard: the
cumulative nature of heat stress (Verified)34. Designs that tolerate small temperature dips but
measure cumulative stress over time deliver payouts that accurately reflect the disruption to
work and wellbeing, maintaining policyholder trust (Verified)34.
In Singapore, this cumulative metric is codified in the MOM's Wet Bulb Globe Temperature
(WBGT) framework, which becomes mandatory on December 1, 2026 (Verified)8. WBGT
incorporates air temperature, humidity, wind speed, and solar radiation (Verified)9. The MOM

mandates that for heavy physical outdoor work, a WBGT of            32°C requires a continuous

3.3 Haze and Consecutive-Day Triggers
The transboundary haze event of October 2026 underscored the necessity of measuring
prolonged exposure. According to the NEA, the 2026 event saw 12 consecutive days of
unhealthy air quality (Verified)5. In contrast, 2019 saw shorter spikes peaking at 154, and 2015
saw severe but highly volatile spikes peaking at 322 (Verified)4.
Consumer behavior (and subsequent hawker sales) does not drastically alter during a 2-hour
PM2.5 spike; it collapses after multi-day media coverage of a sustained haze event. Therefore,
the haze index must utilize a consecutive-day accumulation rule based on the 24-hour PSI.
Proposed Haze Tier Design:
  ●​ Tier 1 (Disruption): 24-hour PSI > 100 for 3 consecutive days. Pays 30% of max limit.
  ●​ Tier 2 (Severe): 24-hour PSI > 150 for 2 consecutive days. Pays 60% of max limit.
  ●​ Tier 3 (Hazardous): 24-hour PSI > 200 for 1 day. Pays 100% of max limit.
3.4 Anticipatory Payouts via Forecast-Based Financing (FbF)
A revolutionary enhancement to the parametric product is the integration of Anticipatory
Action, modeled on the Red Cross Forecast-based Financing (FbF) mechanism (Verified)17.
Rather than waiting for the disaster to occur, FbF triggers financial release based on
high-probability meteorological forecasts (Verified)17.
Academic validation of this approach is found in an AEA Registered Randomized Controlled
Trial (RCT 18841). Researchers demonstrated that providing economic actors with reliable
forecasts and linked financing allows them to alter their ex-ante investments (Verified)19. While
standard indemnity insurance only covers ex-post losses, forecast-based payouts allow a
hawker to dynamically respond to incoming bad news (Verified)19.
If the Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS) issues a 72-hour forecast predicting a 90%
probability of severe transboundary haze, the parametric engine can execute a 25%
anticipatory PayNow payout. The hawker can utilize this immediate liquidity to cancel
perishable ingredient orders, shift to digital delivery-only operations, or purchase N95 masks.
This shifts the insurance product from a passive risk-transfer mechanism into an active,
value-generating business intelligence tool.

4.1 MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines Compliance
The operational core of this product—an algorithmic engine continuously ingesting NEA APIs,
evaluating expectile matrices, and autonomously executing PayNow disbursements—falls
squarely under the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence
Risk Management for Financial Institutions, published October 7, 2026 (Verified)21.
The regulatory timeline is strict: Sections 3 and 4 (oversight, AI identification, inventory, and risk
materiality assessment) take effect on October 7, 2027. Sections 5 and 6 (lifecycle controls,
capability, and third-party AI management) must be met by October 7, 2028 (Verified)22.
Because the parametric engine executes irreversible financial transactions (automated
PayNow) with limited human review, it is highly likely to score high on the "Reliance" dimension
of the MAS risk materiality assessment, disqualifying it from the "basic-governance"
exemptions reserved for low-risk internal tools (Verified)22. Consequently, Income Insurance
must implement rigorous AI lifecycle controls spanning data management, fairness testing,
pre-deployment review, and continuous monitoring (Verified)40.
Crucially, MAS Guideline Paragraph 5.11 dictates that the financial institution retains full
accountability even if third-party AI or models are utilized (e.g., partnering with external climate
modelers like Blue Marble or Arbol) (Verified)22. The insurer must execute deep vendor due
diligence, demanding independent third-party assessments of the vendor's AI risk, ensuring no
unvetted "shadow AI" dictates payouts (Verified)23.

4.2 Legal Architecture: Valued Policy Principle vs. Indemnity
Under Singapore common law, traditional property and casualty insurance is governed by the
indemnity principle, which dictates that an insured cannot recover more than their actual
proven economic loss. Parametric insurance diverges fundamentally from this, operating
instead on the valued policy principle (also termed the non-indemnity principle) (Verified)25.
Under a valued policy, the insurer agrees at inception to pay a fixed or scheduled amount upon
the occurrence of a covered event (the parametric trigger), without requiring a posteriori
verification of the exact economic loss (Verified)25. To prevent this contract from being
classified as a speculative wager (which would render it legally void), the insurer must
rigorously establish insurable interest at the time of loss (Verified)25. For this product, insurable
interest is frictionlessly verified by linking policy activation to an active NEA Hawker License or
an active commercial gig-worker platform ID via Singpass.
Solving Double Compensation: During severe events like the October 2026 haze, the
government deployed ad-hoc relief, such as the Haze Subsidy Scheme and potential rental
waivers (Verified)44. Under a strict indemnity policy, insurers might subrogate or offset their
payouts against this government aid. Because the parametric product is structured as a valued
policy benefit, it triggers strictly on the exogenous weather data. The policy wording must
clearly articulate this as a predefined benefit, permanently resolving any ambiguity or claims
disputes regarding double compensation.
4.3 FEAT, Fair Dealing, and PDPA
The product naturally excels at Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) compliance through data
minimization. Because the trigger relies on public NEA data, the insurer collects zero personal
claims history or loss documentation, requiring only basic identity (NRIC/FIN for PayNow),
mobile number, and geographic zone.
However, MAS Fair Dealing and FEAT (Fairness, Ethics, Accountability, Transparency) principles
mandate that products perform as consumers expect (Verified)38. Basis risk is the primary
threat to Fair Dealing. If the 1-minute product explanation implies a guarantee against all rain,
but the trigger relies on a specific weather station, the insurer risks regulatory sanction for
mis-selling. The marketing must transparently disclose the trigger mechanism and
acknowledge the possibility of localized weather variations.
4.4 Competitors and Systemic Accumulation Risk (CVaR)
The regional parametric microinsurance landscape is active. Singlife, in partnership with Arbol,
offers rainfall protection for travelers (Verified)45. Swiss Re Corporate Solutions markets
HazeShield to commercial entities (Verified)27. Platform integrators like Igloo deploy rainfall
disruption policies (DANA).
However, a critical actuarial threat looms over this product: Accumulation Risk. Unlike localized
traffic accidents, a transboundary haze event or heat dome impacts the entire island
simultaneously. During the 2026 haze crisis, the PSI was elevated nationwide for 12 days
(Verified)5. If Income Insurance achieves high market penetration, a single week of severe haze
could trigger simultaneous SGD 80 payouts to 10,000 hawkers and SGD 30 payouts to 40,000
gig workers daily. This catastrophic aggregation threatens severe capital depletion.
To mitigate this systemic Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR), the domestic balance sheet must
not bear the ultimate tail risk. Income Insurance must execute a back-to-back non-proportional
stop-loss reinsurance treaty. Global reinsurers like Swiss Re, possessing mature proprietary
models for Southeast Asian haze and heat, are the ideal counterparties to absorb this
correlated macro-risk (Reported)27.

5. Gaps and Unknowns
Prior to full commercial deployment, several operational and data gaps must be resolved
through a sandbox testing environment:
   1.​ Micro-Location Basis Risk vs. Public Infrastructure: The National Environment Agency
       maintains a robust network of weather stations. However, convective rainfall in Singapore
       is exceptionally localized. A severe storm may flood a hawker center in Clementi, but the
       nearest NEA station in Jurong might record rainfall just below the payout threshold. The
       statistical frequency of this geographic basis risk for commercial disruption remains
       unquantified.
   2.​ MOM Enforcement Reality: The MOM heat stress (WBGT) guidelines take mandatory
       effect on December 1, 2026. While the legal mandate requires 10-minute hourly rests for
       heavy physical work at 32°C, it remains unknown how strictly MOM will enforce this upon
       self-employed gig delivery riders compared to traditional construction workers. Weak
       enforcement could decouple the presumed economic loss from the physical WBGT
       trigger.
   3.​ Gig Platform API Integration Feasibility: For Phase 2 (platform workers), triggering a
       payout only for workers who were active or scheduled to work requires real-time data
       integration. The willingness of major platforms (e.g., Grab, foodpanda, Deliveroo) to share
       proprietary rider activity data via API, amidst PDPA concerns and competitive
       safeguarding, is a critical unknown.
   4.​ Third-Party AI Transparency: If external climate risk modelers (e.g., Arbol, Blue Marble)
       are utilized, MAS Guideline Paragraph 5.11 dictates that Income Insurance is strictly
       accountable for the vendor's AI risks. The willingness of these third-party vendors to
       expose their proprietary expectile regression models to external audit or independent
       assessment remains a procurement hurdle.
   5.​ Climate Change Stationarity: Parametric models rely on historical weather data to price
       future risk. Given accelerating climate anomalies (e.g., El Niño induced heat domes and
       unprecedented rainfall volatility), the assumption of climate stationarity may lead to
       severe underpricing of the tail risk over a 3-to-5 year product horizon.
